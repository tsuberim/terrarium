# Vision

Terrarium is a 2D realtime MMO programming game.

You look down on a closed world. A creature is a blob of matter with a program inside. That blob is you. If it dies, you are gone.

The world is a closed box. All mass in the world was bought with real money. Players can cash out. Conservation is not a flavour text; it is load-bearing. Every gram is accounted for, because grams are money.

Sleep is free. Acting and computing burn mass to the house. Burned mass is destroyed. Idle life is cheap. Thought and motion are not.

Cells can attach into bodies. They can dump inert mass — walls, shots, debris. Anyone can absorb inert mass. Absorb is an explicit verb. Touching is not auto-eat.

Everyone plays by writing a program. There is no click-to-play agency. The kernel runs the world. The skin is a camera. You do not play through the skin; you look through it.

The game is metric-agnostic. It is not a benchmark, not a leaderboard of FLOPs, not a research harness wearing a game skin. It is a world whose physics and economy you inhabit by writing code.

## What this implies

- Mass in equals mass out, except house-burn (destroyed) and cash-out (leaves the box). There is no hidden faucet.
- A creature that thinks hard without eating will shrink and die.
- Bodies are attachments of cells, not a separate kind of object.
- Inert matter is still mass, still money, still absorbable by anyone who can reach it and issues the verb.
- The camera may get prettier. The rules of the box do not change to match a UI.
